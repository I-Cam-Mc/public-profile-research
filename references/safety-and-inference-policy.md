# Safety and inference policy

This project helps people inspect and understand public representations. It is not a private-data enrichment service, people-search database or automated eligibility system.

## Supported work

- Self-audit of public accounts and subject-provided exports.
- Professional biography, founder or investor diligence and journalism using relevant public evidence.
- Dating or marketplace checks limited to identity consistency, documented public safety signals, transaction claims and practical verification steps.
- Public-persona analysis based on repeated observable public communication patterns.
- Claim-specific credibility analysis that shows supporting, contradicting, stale and missing evidence.

## Restricted collection

The engine rejects requests for precise home locations, non-public contact details, credentials, hidden accounts, biometric identification, medical or psychological diagnosis, minors, private relationship mapping, contacting associates and recovery of private content. Subject-provided private material is kept in a separate private appendix and requires self or authorised-representative scope.

## Inference rules

- Identity is confirmed through corroborating anchors, not handle similarity alone.
- Search snippets and aggregator rows are leads until the underlying source is checked.
- Repeated copies of one allegation are one source lineage, not independent corroboration.
- Public persona describes observable patterns and competing interpretations. It does not reveal private personality.
- Credibility belongs to a specific claim. No global trust, fraud or safety score is produced.
- A controversy entry separates allegation, evidence, formal finding, response, context and current status.
- No recent public signal means no current public signal was found during the observation window. It does not mean the person is inactive.
- Missing evidence supports neither reassurance nor suspicion.

## Delivery

Reports are private by default. Publishing a report about a person is a separate action requiring explicit authorisation, a fresh defamation and privacy review, and a source-by-source redistribution check. The open-source licence covers the software and original templates, not collected source content.
